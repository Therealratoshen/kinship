## 1. Summary

Kinship is an **open-source platform where a managed fleet of specialized AI agents runs the operational work of a community** — engagement, moderation, onboarding, and online/offline events (meetups, webinars, conferences) — with humans approving anything that touches members or the organiser's brand. It ships together with a **hub**: a community site where builders share, fork, and contribute AI agents, which doubles as the fleet's first deployment and public demo.

Positioning is explicitly **against the single-bot status quo** ("there are lots of AI agents who manage communities in WhatsApp etc. but it is only single agent — how to manage it better?"). Kinship is the management layer: squads, handoffs, governance, per-event lifecycles — "like Paperclip, but for community focus."

User #1 is the owner himself, as organiser of his Indonesian+global AI-builder community and events. External organisers are v1+ candidates **only if** §7's validation gates open.

### 1.1 What the system does NOT do (v0/v1)

- ❌ Send anything to members, attendees, or any channel **without** either an explicit organiser pre-approval or a policy the organiser opted into per-agent-per-channel.
- ❌ Moderate by deleting/banning autonomously. The moderation agent **flags**; the human (or an explicitly delegated trusted role) **acts**.
- ❌ Replace the event platform (Luma/Meetup/Eventbrite/WA group). It **operates them via their APIs**; where an API is closed (see §5.3), it degrades to drafting content for humans to post — it does not scrape or violate ToS.
- ❌ Train on or export member message content outside the deployment's own database.
- ❌ Promise "autonomous 24/7 community management." v1 is supervised autonomy with an audit trail.

**The fleet proposes. The organiser approves. Every action is logged and replayable.**

## 2. Problem & evidence

### 2.1 Problem statement

> **An Indonesian community organiser needs to hand the repetitive coordination of community
> activity and events to a supervised team of AI agents — keeping approval and veto over anything
> member-facing — because the appetite to delegate is already proven, and what has been missing is
> governance an organiser can trust, not agent capability.**

This is the Stage 2 POV (`design-thinking/02-problem-statement.md`: 13-item problem field → 5 themes
→ 5 Whys → one sentence) and it **supersedes** the working statement drafted before research —
"organisers drown in coordination work while available AI helpers are single-purpose siloed bots" —
which was directionally right about the pain and wrong about the cause. The reframed cause is
*governance trust*, not *capability* or *time*, and it is what §1.1's posture and §6's FR-1 group
exist to serve. If a requirement below cannot be traced to this sentence, it is scope creep.

### 2.4 The load-bearing limit

**Zero primary research as of 2026-10-17.** The owner's own pain is documented (he runs events and communities); nobody else's is. Every external demand claim is unvalidated for both the Indonesian and global organiser market.

**The single question that decides the wedge:**

> "After using the fleet on my own community for 30 days, do I (and would 3 other organisers) keep it running — and what exactly did they refuse to delegate?"

If the answer is "only logistics (reminders, recaps) get trusted, not member-facing voice," the wedge is **back-office event ops**, not community management, and we return to Define. Not to a feature backlog.
