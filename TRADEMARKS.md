# Trademarks

The **Kinship** name, the **`kinshipai/…` pack-key namespace**, and the **`kinship` / `kinship-runtime`**
package names are the marks of the Kinship project. They are **not** covered by the [Apache-2.0 licence](LICENSE)
— a licence covers the *code*, never the *name* — and this file is how that boundary is stated.

## What is protected

| Mark | Use |
|---|---|
| **Kinship** | the project, the control plane, the governance core |
| **`kinshipai/…`** | the pack-key namespace — the first segment of every pack key (`kinshipai/bundled/…`) |
| **kinship-runtime** | the npm package name, when there is one |

## What is allowed

- **Using the marks to refer to the project.** Blog posts, talks, articles, conference programmes,
  "built on Kinship", "inspired by Kinship", a fork that says it is a fork of Kinship.
- **Forks, under their own name.** You may fork this project, change it, and ship it commercially.
  That is what the licence grants, and it is the point of the project.
- **Using the marks descriptively.** "A governance layer for community agents" is not a trademark use.

## What is not allowed

- **Publishing a product, pack or service under the Kinship name or a confusingly similar one**, in
  a way that suggests it is this project, is endorsed by it, or is its official successor.
- **Publishing a pack under the `kinshipai/…` namespace** other than this project's own packs. The
  namespace exists so a pack's key says who published it; a fork that claims the namespace makes the
  key a lie.
- **Using the marks in your own product name or domain** in a way that implies a relationship
  (a fork called "Kinship Pro", a domain like `kinship-cloud.com`).

## Why this file exists

Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters.

1. **The licence cannot do this job.** A licence grants rights in the code and says nothing
   about the name. A trademark is the only instrument that covers the mark, and a mark nobody has
   claimed cannot be defended later.
2. **A fork that is clearly a fork is good for the project, and a fork that pretends to be the project
   is not.** The mark is how a reader tells the difference. This file is also the project's own
   instruction to its descendants: if you fork this, **say so, and take your own name.**

## Registering

The marks are **not yet registered** in any jurisdiction. Until they are, this file is a statement of
intent and a notice, not an enforceable claim — which is the honest position to be in while the
project is pre-pilot. Registering is an owner decision with a cost, and it is recorded as an open
question rather than assumed.

**Enforcement.** A trademark complaint here has one route: GitHub's
[DMCA process](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/github-dmca-takedown-policy)
for the repository and the namespace, and the relevant registry's own process for the mark. A
maintainer will act on a clear report; see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for the contact route.
